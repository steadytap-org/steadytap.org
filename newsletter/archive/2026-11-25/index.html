Steady Tap newsletter · November 25, 2026

The Nest app is going away. Here's what to do before January 25

Good morning, and happy Thanksgiving week. Inside this week’s edition: a change coming for anyone with a Nest thermostat or camera, a charity scam to watch for before Giving Tuesday, and an easy way to let family know you’re on your way. Whatever your plans are this week, we hope they’re warm and calm.

The Big Story

Have a Nest thermostat or camera? The Nest app is going away

What happened: On November 20, Google said it will close the Nest app on January 25, 2027. The Nest website (home.nest.com) closes too. Nest thermostats, cameras, doorbells, smoke alarms, and locks will move to the free Google Home app instead.

Why it matters: Your Nest devices will keep working in your home. Google says no Nest device is losing support. But to use them from your phone, you’ll need the Google Home app and a Google Account. Still sign in to the Nest app with an old Nest password? You need to move that account. Google’s last day for that is March 25, 2027. After that, Google starts deleting old Nest accounts that weren’t moved.

What to do:

  1. Open the Nest app and look at how you sign in. If you use Sign in with Google, go to step 2. If you use Sign in with Nest and a separate password, tap Settings, then Account, then Migrate to a Google Account. Google says you can’t undo this, and people who share your home may need a new invite.
  2. Get the free Google Home app from the App Store or Play Store. Check that the maker is Google. Sign in with your Google Account.
  3. Have an older Nest camera with saved clips? On a computer, go to home.nest.com before January 25 and use Download All. Saved clips won’t move to Google Home.

Moving is free. If someone says you must pay to keep your thermostat working, it’s a scam.

Sources: Google Home & Nest Help: Nest app transition to the Google Home app: https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/18959693?hl=en | Google Home & Nest Community: Changes coming to Google Assistant and Nest app (Nov. 20, 2026): https://support.google.com/googlehome/thread/478236207?hl=en | Google Home & Nest Help: Migrate your Nest account to a Google Account: https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/9297676?hl=en

Scam Watch

The fake charity ask before Giving Tuesday

Example of a scam text (not real, do not reply):

“Thank you for your kind gift last year! Families need help this holiday. Please give again before midnight. Donate with a gift card here: help-families-fund-dot-org/give”

Why it works: Giving Tuesday, the Tuesday after Thanksgiving, is December 1 this year. Many people give at this time of year, and scammers know it. The FTC says some scammers use names that sound a lot like real charities. Some thank you for a gift you never made. And they rush you. On November 18, the U.S. Treasury named charity scams as one of the most common holiday scams.

The one thing to do: Don’t give through a link in a text, a call, or an ad. Pick the charity yourself and type its web address. Search its name with the word “complaint” or “scam” first. The FTC says to pay by credit card or check. If anyone wants cash, a gift card, or a wire transfer, don’t do it. That’s how scammers ask to be paid.

Where to report: Tell the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). You can also tell your state charity regulator. Find yours at nasconet.org (https://www.nasconet.org/). If you sent money, call your bank and report it to the FBI at ic3.gov (https://www.ic3.gov/).

Sources: FTC: Donating safely and avoiding scams: https://consumer.ftc.gov/features/donating-safely-and-avoiding-scams | U.S. Treasury: Annual consumer advisory on holiday scams (Nov. 18, 2026): https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0694 | IRS: Tax Exempt Organization Search: https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search

Quick Hits

  • Scam losses reported by older adults keep growing. A new FTC report says losses reported by people 60 and over rose from $600 million in 2020 to about $3 billion in 2025. Older adults were more likely to lose money to tech support, prize, and government imposter scams. (FTC: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/new-report-ftcs-work-protect-older-adults)

  • Your 2027 Social Security letter may be online now. Benefits go up 3.6% in January. If you chose paperless notices, look for your letter in the Message Center of your my Social Security account starting in late November. If not, watch your mail. (SSA: https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/)

  • Medicare Open Enrollment ends December 7. Want a different Medicare Advantage or drug plan for 2027? Your new plan must get your request by December 7. Compare plans for free at Medicare.gov, or call 1-800-MEDICARE. (Medicare.gov: https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/open-enrollment)

  • Real FTC websites end in .gov. The FTC says scammers make copycat FTC sites that end in .com, .org, .us, or .online. A real FTC worker will never tell you to move your money to “protect” it. (FTC: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/how-spot-fake-ftc-websites-and-ftc-impersonators)

One Small Thing to Try

Let family follow your holiday trip

Driving or flying to see family this week? You can let one person you trust see where you are, so they know you’re on your way. It takes about 2 minutes, and you can stop anytime.

iPhone (Find My app):

  1. Open the Find My app. Tap Me at the bottom and turn on Share My Location.
  2. Tap People, then tap the Add button (+), then tap Share My Location.
  3. Pick the person you want to share with.
  4. Choose how long: for an hour, until the end of the day, or a time you pick. Both of you need an iPhone for this.

Android (Google Maps app):

  1. Open Google Maps. Make sure you’re signed in to your Google Account.
  2. Tap your profile picture or initial, then tap Location sharing, then New share.
  3. Choose how long you want to share.
  4. Tap the person, then tap Share. They need a Gmail account. If they don’t have one, tap Copy to clipboard and text them the link. A link works for up to 24 hours.

To stop on iPhone: in Find My, tap People, tap the person, tap More, then scroll down and tap Stop Sharing My Location. In Google Maps: tap your picture, then Location sharing, tap the person, and tap Stop. Only share with people you know and trust.

Sources: Apple: Share your location with iPhone: https://support.apple.com/en-us/105104 | Google: Share your real-time location in Google Maps: https://support.google.com/maps/answer/15437054?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en

A Question We Hear a Lot

“Family is visiting. How do I give them my Wi-Fi password?”

You may not have to read out a long password at all.

iPhone to iPhone: Have your guest tap your Wi-Fi name on their iPhone or iPad. Keep your iPhone unlocked, on Wi-Fi, and close to theirs. When your phone asks, tap Share Password, then Done. It works best when you each have the other saved in Contacts, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on.

Android: Open Settings, tap Network & internet, then Internet. Tap your Wi-Fi network, then tap Share. Your phone shows a QR code (a square barcode). Your guest points their phone’s camera at it to join. Samsung and some other phones look a little different.

If it doesn’t work, your guest can still type the password in by hand.

Sources: Apple: How to share your Wi-Fi password from your iPhone or iPad: https://support.apple.com/en-us/102635 | Google: Connect to and share Wi-Fi networks on Android: https://support.google.com/android/answer/9075847?hl=en
