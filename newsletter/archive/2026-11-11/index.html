One week left to see your Social Security letter online

Good morning, and welcome to this week’s edition. Today is Veterans Day, so thank you to everyone who served, and to the families who served with them. Inside: a Social Security deadline that’s one week away, a fake VA benefits message to watch for, and a 2-minute phone setting that could help in an emergency.

The Big Story

Want to read your Social Security raise letter early? Turn on paperless by November 18

What happened: Each year, Social Security sends a letter called the COLA notice. It shows your new benefit amount for next year. (COLA means cost-of-living adjustment, the yearly raise. For 2027 it’s 3.6%.) This year’s letter shows up online in late November. To see it online, you need a free my Social Security account with paperless notices turned on by November 18. That’s one week from today.

Why it matters: Online, you can read your letter up to three weeks before the paper copy arrives. The letter is also simpler this year: one page, in plain words, with your exact new amount and any money taken out. If you have Medicare, your 2027 Medicare premium shows up in the same place, called the Message Center.

What to do:

  1. Type ssa.gov/myaccount into your web browser yourself. Don’t use a link from a text or email.
  2. No account yet? Make one with Login.gov or ID.me. Have your email, a photo ID, and a phone ready. It takes 5 to 20 minutes.
  3. Sign in. Tap the My Profile tab, then Notification Preferences.
  4. Turn Paperless Communications to ON. Read the agreement, then tap Agree and Continue.
  5. You can also ask for a text or email alert when a new letter is ready.

Rather have paper? That’s fine. Do nothing, and your letter comes by mail in December. Either way, the raise itself is automatic. Questions? Call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213.

Sources: SSA: 3.6% benefit increase for 2027 (Oct. 14, 2026): https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/press/releases/2026-10-14.html | SSA: Opt out of mailed notices (paperless steps): https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/opt-out.html | SSA: Creating an account: https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/creating-an-account.html

Scam Watch

The “pay a fee to file your VA claim” message

Example of a scam text (not real, do not reply):

“VETERANS DAY BENEFITS ALERT: You may qualify for a bigger VA disability payment. Our claims team can file for you today. A $199 filing fee is due now. Reply YES or visit va-claim-boost-dot-com”

Why it works: Veterans Day puts VA benefits in the news, and the benefits are real. Scammers use that to sound helpful. They push you to act “today” so you don’t stop and check.

The one thing to do: VA never charges a fee to apply for VA benefits or VA health care. Don’t sign a blank form, and don’t agree to pay anyone a share of your benefits for help with a claim. To check a message, type VA.gov yourself or call VA at 1-800-827-1000. Not a veteran? Please pass this on to one you know.

Where to report: Call VA’s VSAFE fraud hotline at 833-38V-SAFE (833-388-7233), and tell the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

Sources: VA: Protect your identity and your VA benefits from scammers (updated Oct. 23, 2026): https://www.va.gov/resources/how-to-protect-your-identity-and-your-va-benefits-from-scammers/ | VA: VSAFE: https://www.vsafe.gov/

Quick Hits

  • A new iPhone update is out. On November 9, Apple released iOS 27.2.1 with security fixes, including for Bluetooth and AirPlay. Older iPhones got updates too. To install, open Settings, tap General, then Software Update. (Apple: https://support.apple.com/en-us/149715)

  • Older adults lost about $3 billion to fraud. A new FTC report says fraud losses reported by people 60 and over grew from $600 million in 2020 to about $3 billion in 2025. Older adults were more likely to lose money to tech support, prize, and government imposter scams. (FTC: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/new-report-ftcs-work-protect-older-adults)

  • Booking holiday travel? Scroll before you pay. The FTC warns that some booking sites hide extra charges below the “Book” or “Pay” button. Scroll all the way down, uncheck add-ons you didn’t choose, and compare the final total to the first price you saw. (FTC: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/booking-holiday-travel-hidden-fees-can-cost-you)

  • Have Medicare? You don’t need the Marketplace. HealthCare.gov’s sign-up time is for people without health insurance. It’s against the law for someone who knows you have Medicare to sell you a Marketplace plan. Medicare’s own Open Enrollment ends December 7. (Medicare.gov: https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/other-paths/medicare-marketplace)

One Small Thing to Try

Put your medical info on your phone’s lock screen

If you ever need help, a first responder can see your allergies, medicines, and emergency contacts right from your lock screen, without your passcode. It takes about 2 minutes to set up.

iPhone:

  1. Open the Health app.
  2. Tap your picture in the top corner, then tap Medical ID.
  3. Tap Get Started or Edit. Add things like your medicines, allergies, and health conditions.
  4. Under Emergency Contacts, tap Add Emergency Contact and pick someone.
  5. Scroll to the bottom. Turn on Show When Locked and Emergency Call, then tap Done.

Samsung Galaxy:

  1. Open Settings, then tap Safety and emergency.
  2. Tap Medical info. Fill in things like conditions, allergies, and current medicines. Tap Save.
  3. Go back and tap Emergency contacts, then Add emergency contact.

Other Android phones (like Google Pixel):

  1. Open the Safety app and sign in to your Google Account.
  2. Tap Your info, then Medical information, and fill it in.
  3. Tap Emergency contacts, then Add contact.
  4. Tap Emergency info access, then Show when locked.

Remember: anyone holding your phone can see this info, even when it’s locked. Add only what you’d want a first responder to know.

Sources: Apple: Set up your Medical ID: https://support.apple.com/en-us/105072 | Samsung: Use your Galaxy phone in emergency situations: https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001579/ | Google: Get help during an emergency with your Android phone: https://support.google.com/android/answer/9319337

A Question We Hear a Lot

“My son offered to set up my Social Security account for me, using his email. Is that OK?”

It’s kind of him to help, and having family sit beside you is a great idea. But the account itself should be yours. Social Security says each person needs their own account with their own email address. Don’t share your account or email with anyone, even a spouse or family member.

No email yet? You can make a free one first, then use it for your account.

Only have a home phone? That works too. Login.gov can send your sign-in code by a call to your landline.

One more tip: those sign-in codes are only for you. Never read one out to someone who calls you.

Sources: SSA: my Social Security account FAQs and help: https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/account-faqs-and-help.html
