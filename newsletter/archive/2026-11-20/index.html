Steady Tap newsletter · November 20, 2026

Medicare's 2027 costs are out. Here's how to compare plans by Dec 7

Good morning, and welcome to this week’s edition. This week’s paper is reaching you a couple of days later than usual, so thank you for your patience. Inside: Medicare’s new 2027 costs and the deadline to compare plans, a fake toll text that’s going around, and a 2-minute way to quiet spam calls.

The Big Story

Medicare’s 2027 costs are out, and you have until December 7 to compare plans

What happened: On November 17, Medicare announced its 2027 costs. The standard Part B premium (the monthly fee for doctor and outpatient care) goes from $202.90 to $209.40 in January. The yearly Part B deductible goes from $283 to $292. Medicare says the change is mainly because health care spending is going up. People with higher incomes may pay more.

Why it matters: Medicare sets the Part B premium. Your own plan’s costs are separate, and they can change each year too. Open Enrollment, the time each year to switch plans, ends December 7. Changes start January 1. Happy with your plan? You don’t have to do anything. Comparing is still free and takes a little while.

What to do:

  1. Find the yearly letter from your plan. It’s called the Annual Notice of Change. It lists what’s different next year.
  2. Type Medicare.gov/plan-compare into your browser yourself. Don’t use a link from a text, email, or ad.
  3. Enter your ZIP code, or log in to your Medicare account. Add your drugs and your pharmacy so you can see your yearly costs.
  4. Compare the plans side by side. If you switch, the plan must get your request by December 7.
  5. Want a person to help? Call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227), or your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP). It’s free.

Never give your Medicare number to someone who calls you first. Real help is free.

Sources: CMS: 2027 Medicare Parts A & B premiums and deductibles (Nov. 17, 2026): https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2027-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles | Medicare.gov: What does Medicare cost?: https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/what-does-medicare-cost | Medicare.gov: Open Enrollment (Oct. 15 to Dec. 7): https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/open-enrollment

Scam Watch

The “unpaid toll” text

Example of a scam text (not real, do not reply):

“Final notice: You have an unpaid toll balance of $12.51 on your record. Pay today to avoid a $50 late fee: toll-pay-service-dot-com/pay”

Why it works: Many roads now use cameras instead of toll booths, so a toll bill seems believable. The amount is small on purpose. The late fee and the rush are meant to make you tap before you think. A new AARP survey found 44% of adults got a fake toll text in the past year.

The one thing to do: Don’t tap the link or reply. The FCC says toll companies don’t usually text to collect overdue bills. To check, type your toll company’s web address yourself, or call the number on your toll account. Then forward the text to 7726 (it spells SPAM) and delete it.

Where to report: Tell the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). If you lost money or tapped the link, also tell the FBI at ic3.gov (https://www.ic3.gov/).

Sources: FCC: How to spot and avoid toll road payment scam texts: https://www.fcc.gov/consumer-governmental-affairs/how-spot-and-avoid-toll-road-payment-scam-texts | FBI IC3: Smishing scam regarding debt for road toll services: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240412 | AARP: Holiday shopping and scams survey (Nov. 11, 2026): https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2026-11-11-how-to-avoid-fraud-and-scams-this-holiday-season-aarp-breaks-down-what-to-watch-out-for.html

Quick Hits

  • Your 2027 Social Security letter is on its way. Benefits go up 3.6% in January. If you turned on paperless by November 18, your letter appears in the Message Center of your my Social Security account starting in late November. Everyone else gets a paper letter in early December. (SSA: https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/mip/2026-10-01-f.html)

  • Shopping for gifts? Scroll past the top search results. The FTC says scammers pay for search ads that can sit at the very top. Scroll down to the regular results, or type the store’s web address yourself. (FTC: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/how-avoid-holiday-shopping-scams-your-search-results)

  • A “grandkid” calls and asks for money? Hang up. The FTC says some scammers copy a loved one’s voice. Hang up, then call that person back or call another family member to check. Never pay with a gift card, wire transfer, crypto, or payment app. (FTC: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/grandpa-i-need-your-help-how-spot-and-avoid-grandkid-scams)

  • Giving a gift card? Check it first. AARP found that 35% of adults have given or received a gift card with no money on it. Before you buy one, check that the packaging hasn’t been opened. Our free guide shows how. (AARP: https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2026-11-11-how-to-avoid-fraud-and-scams-this-holiday-season-aarp-breaks-down-what-to-watch-out-for.html)

One Small Thing to Try

Quiet the spam calls

Your phone can warn you about spam calls and keep many of them from ringing. It takes about 2 minutes. First, save the numbers of family, friends, and your doctor in Contacts, so their calls always ring.

iPhone:

  1. Open Settings, then tap Apps, then tap Phone.
  2. Scroll to Screen Unknown Callers. Tap Ask Reason for Calling. Your iPhone asks unknown callers why they’re calling before it rings.
  3. Scroll down to Call Filtering and turn on your Spam filter. Likely spam calls move to a Spam list.
  4. You can see these calls later. In the Phone app, tap Filter, then Spam.

Android (Phone by Google app):

  1. Open the Phone app. Tap More options (three dots), then Settings.
  2. Tap Caller ID and spam.
  3. Make sure See caller ID & spam is on.
  4. If you see Filter spam calls, turn it on. Filtered calls still show in your call history.

Samsung and some other phones look a little different. Ask a family member to help, or see our guide. Also remember: if a call still gets through and asks for money, hang up.

Sources: Apple: Manage unknown callers on iPhone: https://support.apple.com/en-us/111106 | Google: Use caller ID & spam protection: https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/3459196

A Question We Hear a Lot

“How do I make the words on my phone bigger?”

It takes about two minutes, and you can always change it back.

iPhone: Open Settings, tap Display & Brightness, then tap Text Size and drag the slider right. Want it bigger still? Go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size, then Larger Text.

Android: Open Settings, search for Font size, and drag the slider right. Search for Display size to make everything bigger. Not every app follows this setting, and that’s normal.

If buttons get hard to tap, move the slider back one step.

Sources: Apple: Change the font size on your iPhone: https://support.apple.com/en-us/102453 | Google: Change text & display settings on Android: https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/11183305
