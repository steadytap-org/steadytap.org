What’s the Difference Between Streaming, Cable, and Satellite TV?

What this helps with: Understanding the ways to get TV at home, so you can pick what fits you and stop paying for what you don’t use.

There are four main ways to get TV. Many homes use more than one. Here’s what each one is, in plain words.

The four ways to get TV

  • Antenna (free). A small antenna picks up your local channels out of the air, like ABC, CBS, FOX, NBC, and PBS. There’s no monthly bill. You need a TV made in the last 15 years or so, or a converter box.
  • Cable. A company runs a wire to your home and you pay each month for a package of channels. You often rent a cable box too.
  • Satellite. A dish on your roof or wall gets channels from space. Like cable, you pay each month and usually rent a box.
  • Streaming. Shows come over your home internet into an app, like Netflix, YouTube, PBS, or Tubi. Some apps are free. Others cost a monthly fee. You need internet and a smart TV or a small streaming device.

Good to know: “Live TV” streaming services (like YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, or Sling) carry cable channels over the internet. They work like cable, but without the box or the wire.

Which one is right for you?

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. What do you watch most? Write down your five favorite channels or shows.
  2. Are they on free TV? Local news, many sports games, and many popular shows are on your local channels, which you can get with an antenna.
  3. Do you have home internet? Streaming needs it. If you already pay for internet, streaming apps may cost less than cable.

Local news and network showsAn antenna (free)
Movies and older showsFree streaming apps, or one paid app
Many cable channels and live sportsCable, satellite, or a live TV streaming service

Save money: simple steps

  1. Find your real total. Cable and satellite companies must now show one “all-in” price for their TV service on your bill, according to the FCC. Taxes and box rental can still be extra. Add it all up.
  2. Check for free local channels. Type fcc.gov/media/engineering/dtvmaps into your web browser and enter your address. It shows which channels an antenna can pick up where you live.
  3. List what you really watch. Then compare it to your package. You may be paying for many channels you never turn on.
  4. Count your boxes. Each extra box often costs money each month. Return the ones you don’t use.
  5. Try before you switch. Many streaming apps are free, or offer a short free trial. Write down when a trial ends so you’re not charged by surprise.

For more ways to cut your bill, see How Do I Save Money on Cable and Streaming TV?

Stay safe

  • Be careful with “TV support” phone numbers you find by searching. Scammers set up fake ones. Use the number on your bill or the company’s own website.
  • Your TV or streaming device never needs an “activation fee” paid by gift card or to a stranger. That’s a scam.
