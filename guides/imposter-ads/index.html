Is This Ad Really From the Company? How to Spot Imposter Ads

What this helps with: Spotting fake ads that pretend to be a real company or a government agency. They show up in your social media feed, in search results, and on shopping sites.

The short answer: Don’t trust an ad just because it has a famous name on it. To reach a company or agency, type its web address yourself, or use its app. Get phone numbers from your bill, your card, or the official website. Scroll past results marked Ad or Sponsored.

On Nov. 2, 2026, the FTC warned that scammers now pay for ads, just like real companies do. Their ads use the names of well-known businesses and government agencies. The FTC says people are losing big money. Here’s how to tell, and what to do.

What an imposter ad looks like

  • It’s at the very top of your search. You search for a company’s phone number or website. The first results are ads, marked Ad or Sponsored. Anyone can pay for those spots, including scammers.
  • It uses a real name and logo. It may say it’s from your bank, a big store, a tech company, or an agency like Social Security or Medicare.
  • It has a “help line” number. You call, and the person asks you to pay, share private details, or let them into your computer.
  • The web address is a little off. The name is spelled wrong, or has extra words, like “amazon-help-desk” instead of “amazon.com.”
  • It wants you to act fast. “Fix your account now.” “Last chance.” “Claim your refund today.”
  • It asks for a strange kind of payment. A real company or agency won’t ask for a gift card, a wire transfer, or crypto.

What to do

  1. Pause before you tap. An ad in your feed or search results isn’t proof that it’s real.
  2. Scroll past the ads. In search results, skip anything marked Ad or Sponsored. Look for the company’s own website below them.
  3. Type the address yourself, or open the app you already have. For example, type ssa.gov, medicare.gov, or your bank’s address into the bar at the top.
  4. Get phone numbers from a place you trust. Use the number on the back of your card, on your bill or statement, or on the official website you typed in.
  5. Hang up if a “help line” asks for money or access. Real support won’t ask for a gift card or for control of your computer.
    • What you’ll tap: The red button to end the call.
  6. Report the ad. This helps the site take it down.
    • Google: On the ad, tap More (three dots) or Info. Then tap Report ad and pick a reason.
    • Facebook or Instagram: Tap the three dots on the ad, then Report ad. The words can be a little different in each app.

If you already called or paid

  • I only tapped the ad. Close the page. Don’t type anything in. It’s a good idea to update your phone so it has the newest safety fixes.
  • I paid or gave my card number. Call your bank or card company right away. Use the number on the back of your card. Tell them it was a scam. Ask them to stop the payment.
  • I let someone into my computer. Turn off the internet on that computer. Then change your passwords from a different device. Two-step login adds extra protection.
  • I gave out my Social Security number. Go to IdentityTheft.gov for a step-by-step plan.
  • Someone offers to get my money back for a fee. That is usually a second scam. Don’t pay.
  • It’s not your fault. These ads fool careful people. Telling a family member can help.

Where to report it

  • Any imposter ad or scam: Tell the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • A fake Google ad: Use Google’s report an ad steps.
  • Online scams where you lost money: Report to the FBI at IC3.gov.
  • Free help talking it through: Call the AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline at 877-908-3360. You don’t need to be an AARP member.

Want to share your views? The FTC is asking the public if sites should check ads before they run and remove fake ones. You can comment until Nov. 30, 2026. It’s optional. Details are in the FTC alert below.

Outside the US?

The reporting services named in this guide are for the United States. If you live elsewhere, report scams to your country’s official service:

  • United Kingdom: Report Fraud (formerly Action Fraud). England, Wales and Northern Ireland. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101.
  • Canada: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre
  • Australia: Scamwatch
  • Ireland: An Garda Síochána: Fraud
  • New Zealand: Netsafe (scams)
  • New Zealand: National Cyber Security Centre (formerly CERT NZ)

In an emergency, call your local emergency number (for example 999 or 112 in the UK and Ireland, 000 in Australia, 111 in New Zealand, or 911 in Canada).
