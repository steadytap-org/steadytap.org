Holiday Travel Checklist: How Do I Get My Phone Ready for a Trip?

What this helps with: Getting your phone ready before you travel for Thanksgiving or the holidays: your boarding pass, flight alerts, chargers, sharing your trip with family, and what to do if a flight is canceled or a bag is lost.

The short answer: A few days before you go, update your phone and get the airline’s own app. Save your boarding pass to your phone’s wallet. Pack your charger and any battery pack in your carry-on bag. Share your trip with one family member. And still bring your physical ID.

Thanksgiving is November 26, 2026. Airports get very busy around the holiday. A little setup at home saves stress in line.

A few days before you go

  1. Update your phone. Updates fix problems and close security holes. See How to Update Your Phone. Do it at home on Wi-Fi, not at the airport.
  2. Get the airline’s own app. Open the App Store (iPhone) or Play Store (Android). Type the airline’s name and check that the maker is the airline. Sign in with the confirmation number from your booking email.
  3. Turn on flight alerts. When the app asks to send notifications, tap Allow. Then you’ll hear about gate changes and delays right away.
  4. Check your ID. Adults need an acceptable ID at airport security, like a REAL ID driver’s license or a passport. TSA lists them all on its website. Some airports also take a digital ID in Apple Wallet, Google Wallet, or Samsung Wallet. That is optional, and TSA says you must still carry your physical ID. No REAL ID or passport? TSA charges a $45 fee, called TSA ConfirmID, to try to confirm who you are. It may not work, and the fee is not refunded. Pay only through Pay.gov, using the link on tsa.gov/confirmid, not a link from a text or ad.

Put your boarding pass on your phone

First, check in for your flight in the airline’s app or on its website. Then save the boarding pass to your phone’s wallet.

On iPhone:

  1. Open the airline app or the boarding pass email.
  2. Tap Add to Apple Wallet.
  3. Tap Add if asked.
  4. At the airport, open the Wallet app and tap your boarding pass. Hold it up to the scanner, or show it to the agent.

On Android:

  1. Open the airline app, email, or website with your boarding pass.
  2. Tap Add to Google Wallet.
  3. At the airport, open Google Wallet and tap your pass. Show the code to be scanned.

Tip: Not every airline offers this. If you don’t see the button, use the pass in the airline app. Many people also bring a paper copy, just in case.

Pack your chargers the right way

  • Chargers and battery packs go in your carry-on bag. TSA says portable chargers and power banks must not go in checked bags.
  • Charge everything the night before: your phone, your battery pack, and hearing aids or earbuds that charge.
  • Bring a long charging cable. Outlets at gates and in hotels can be far from the bed or seat.
  • Battery getting low? Turn on Low Power Mode (iPhone) or Battery Saver (Android). See How Do I Make My Phone Battery Last Longer?

Share your trip with family

Pick one person who will know where you are. Here are free ways to do it.

  • Share your flight (iPhone): In the Wallet app, tap your boarding pass, then tap Share Flight Tracker. Pick a family member in Messages. If they have an iPhone, they can follow your flight’s status.
  • Share your location (iPhone): Open Find My. Tap Me and turn on Share My Location. Then tap People, tap the + button, and tap Share My Location. Pick a person and how long to share, like an hour or until the end of the day.
  • Share your location (Android): Open Google Maps, tap your picture at the top, then tap Location sharing. Pick how long to share and the person to share with.
  • When you get home, you can stop sharing the same way.

Only share with people you know and trust. If someone you don’t know asks you to share your location, say no.

If your bag doesn’t show up

If you put an AirTag or another tracker tag in your bag, it can help.

  • iPhone: Open Find My, tap Items, and tap your bag’s tag. Then tap Share Item Location and follow the steps. This makes a link you can give to the airline. Apple says sharing stops after 7 days or when you get your bag back.
  • Android: Google’s Find Hub app can also share a tracker’s location with some airlines.
  • Always file a report with the airline at its baggage desk or in its app, too.

If your flight is canceled

  • Check the airline app first. It often offers a new flight before the line at the desk moves.
  • You can ask for a refund. The U.S. Department of Transportation says that if an airline cancels your flight and you choose not to travel or take another flight, you’re owed a refund. That includes flights to, from, or within the U.S.
  • Use only the airline’s real app, website, or desk. Scammers pay for ads with fake customer service numbers. Type the airline’s website yourself, or use the number in your booking email. See How to Spot Imposter Ads.

Booking a trip or a hotel? Watch for hidden fees

The FTC warned in November 2026 that some travel sites add extra charges you might not see.

  • Scroll down before you tap “Book” or “Pay.” Look for anything below the button.
  • Look for boxes that are already checked, like a “tip” or “VIP support.” Uncheck anything you didn’t choose.
  • Compare the final total to the first price. If it went up, find out why before you pay.
  • Pay with a credit card when you can. See How Do I Pay Safely When I Shop Online?

Got a message that your hotel payment “failed”? Don’t tap the link. See Is This Hotel or Travel Booking Message a Scam?

Driving this holiday? A text that says you owe an “unpaid toll” is often a scam. See Got a Text About an Unpaid Toll?
