Is This Post Real, or Is It an Ad?

What this helps with: Telling ads from real posts on social media, and checking whether a post, photo, or story is true before you believe or share it.

Your feed is a mix of posts from friends, posts from pages you follow, posts the app picks for you, and ads. They can look alike. Here’s how to tell them apart.

Spot the ad: look for “Sponsored”

Companies pay to put ads in your feed. The apps label them.

  • Facebook and Instagram: ads say Sponsored under the name at the top of the post.
  • YouTube: ads say Sponsored or Ad, often with a countdown to skip.
  • X: ads say Ad in the corner.
  • Nextdoor: ads say Sponsored.

Good to know: An ad isn’t always bad. But a deal that looks too good, or a famous person “recommending” a product, is often a scam. Famous faces are sometimes used in fake ads without their permission.

To see why you got an ad on Facebook or Instagram, tap the three dots on the ad, then Why am I seeing this ad?

Suggested posts aren’t from your friends

Apps also add posts from people and pages you don’t follow. They may say Suggested for you or Follow. These aren’t ads, but they’re not from your friends either.

To see only your friends:

  • Facebook: tap Menu, then Feeds, then Friends.
  • Instagram: tap the Instagram logo at the top, then Following.

Is the post true? Four quick checks

  1. Who posted it? Is it a person or group you know? A brand new page with few posts is a warning sign.
  2. Does it make you feel strong anger, fear, or excitement? Posts made to stir up feelings spread fast, true or not. Pause before sharing.
  3. Can you find it somewhere else? Search for the story on a few well-known news sites. If no one else is reporting it, be careful.
  4. Is the date current? Old stories and photos often get shared again as if they’re new.

Fake and AI-made photos and videos

Computers can now make photos and videos that look real but aren’t. Some apps add a label like AI info or Made with AI, but not always.

Clues a picture may be fake:

  • Hands, teeth, or ears look odd.
  • Text in the picture is blurry or makes no sense.
  • Skin looks too smooth, or lighting looks strange.
  • It’s an amazing scene that no news outlet shows.

Try this: you can search with a photo to see where else it appears. In the Google app, tap the camera icon (Google Lens) and pick the photo. If it shows up years earlier, or on a fact-checking site, it’s not what the post says.

A neutral example: a photo of a “giant wave hitting a city” goes around after a storm. A photo search shows it’s from a movie. That’s a fake, no matter who shared it.

Before you share

Ask yourself: Do I know this is true? If you’re not sure, it’s okay not to share. That alone helps stop false posts from spreading.
