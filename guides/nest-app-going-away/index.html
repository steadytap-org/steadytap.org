The Nest App Is Going Away. What Do I Need to Do?

What this helps with: You use the Nest app for a Nest thermostat, camera, doorbell, smoke alarm (Nest Protect), or Nest x Yale lock. Google is shutting the Nest app down. This guide shows the few steps to take, and what happens to your Google or Nest speaker.

The short answer: Your Nest devices will keep working. You just need to use the Google Home app instead. Before January 25, 2027, get the Google Home app and sign in with a Google Account. If you still sign in to the Nest app with an old Nest password, move that account to a Google Account first. And save any video clips you want to keep. The move is free.

The dates to know

  • January 25, 2027: The Nest app stops working on phones, tablets, TVs, and the web (home.nest.com). Your devices and camera video history start moving to the Google Home app by themselves that day.
  • March 25, 2027: The last day to move an old Nest account to a Google Account. After that, Google starts deleting old Nest accounts that were never moved, with their data.
  • August 2, 2027: Google Assistant stops on Google and Nest speakers and smart displays. They switch to Gemini for Home. You have to say yes to it yourself. See the speaker section below.

Google says no Nest device is losing support because of this. It’s only the app that’s going away.

Step 1: Check how you sign in

  1. Open the Nest app. Go to the screen where you sign in, or tap Settings, then Account.
  2. Look at how you sign in:
    • “Sign in with Google”: You already use a Google Account. Go to Step 2.
    • “Sign in with Nest” and a separate Nest password: You need to move your account once. Keep reading.

Good to know: If you started using the Nest app after August 19, 2019, you already have a Google Account.

To move an old Nest account:

  1. In the Nest app, tap Settings, then Account.
  2. Tap Migrate to a Google Account.
  3. Tap Continue with Google and pick your Google Account. Use the same one you use for Gmail or a Google speaker, if you have one. No Google Account? The screen lets you make a free one.
  4. Follow the steps on the screen, then tap Complete migration.

Before you start: If other people share your Nest home, they may lose access when you move. You can invite them again in the Google Home app. Google also says you can’t undo the move.

What you’ll tap: Settings (a gear), then Account, then Migrate to a Google Account.

Step 2: Get the Google Home app

  1. Open the App Store (iPhone or iPad) or the Play Store (Android).
  2. Search for Google Home. Check that the maker is Google.
  3. Install it. Then sign in with your Google Account.

Your Nest thermostat, smoke alarms, and lock show up in the Google Home app by themselves once your account is a Google Account. Your cameras and doorbells, with their recent video, move over on January 25, 2027. You don’t have to scan any codes again. Google’s help page also shows how to move cameras sooner, if you want to.

Most things work the same: live video, two-way talk, video history, thermostat schedules, smoke alarm alerts, and lock passcodes.

Step 3: Save video clips you want to keep

This matters if you have an older Nest camera or doorbell and saved clips on the Nest website. Those saved clips will not move to the Google Home app. Your recent video history will.

  1. On a computer, go to home.nest.com and sign in.
  2. Use the Download All button to save your clips to the computer.
  3. Do this before January 25, 2027.

Do I have to pay anything?

No. Moving to the Google Home app is free.

  • Nest Aware (1st gen) plan: Google says it switches to a Google Home Premium Advanced plan on January 25, 2027, at no extra cost. You don’t need to type in your card again. Google says it will email you closer to the date.
  • Have ADT? Google says ADT systems don’t use the Nest app, so nothing changes.

Watch out for scams. If a call, text, or pop-up says you must pay to keep your thermostat or camera working, it’s a scam. Don’t call a number from a search ad. Open the Google Home app yourself, or go to Google’s own help page. See How to Spot a Scam Call, Text, or Email.

What about my Google or Nest speaker?

On August 2, 2027, Google Assistant ends on Google Home and Nest speakers and smart displays (like Nest Mini and Nest Hub). They switch to Gemini for Home, a newer voice helper. You can still talk to it in plain words.

  • It’s free. A paid Google Home Premium plan adds extras, like Gemini Live and searching your camera video.
  • It won’t happen by itself. You have to say yes in the Google Home app.
  • If you don’t, the speaker will stop answering after Google Assistant ends. It will ask you to upgrade. You can upgrade at any time to make it work again.

To upgrade:

  1. Open the Google Home app.
  2. Look for a message that says “Introducing Gemini for Home.” Tap it.
  3. Don’t see it? Tap your profile picture (or initial) at the top right. Tap Home settings. On the banner about Gemini for Home, tap Get started.
  4. Follow the steps on the screen.

The upgrade covers all the speakers in your home at once. Google is still bringing Gemini for Home to more countries, so it may not show up yet where you live. Google says it will remind you in the app before August 2027.

Already have Gemini on an Android phone? That’s a separate change. See My Android Phone Has Gemini Instead of Google Assistant. What Changed?

Privacy and safety

  • Your Google Account is now the key to your home. Use a strong password and turn on two-step login.
  • Check who has access. After you move, look at who is in your home in the Google Home app. Remove anyone who shouldn’t be there.
  • Helping a parent? Do the steps together on their phone, with their account. Don’t use your own account for their home.

If something goes wrong

  • “Migrate to a Google Account” isn’t there. Your account has probably moved already. Try signing in to the Google Home app with your Google Account.
  • I don’t see my devices in Google Home. Check that you signed in with the same Google Account you picked when you moved. Thermostats and alarms show up right away. Cameras move on January 25, 2027.
  • I use a work or school Google Account. Google suggests a personal Gmail account instead, because some features may not work.
  • Still stuck? Use Google’s Nest app transition help page. It walks you through each step.
